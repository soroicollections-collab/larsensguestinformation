Welcome & Karibu

Dear Intrepid Traveller,

We are delighted to host you at our boutique camp in the most beautiful setting, on and along the mighty Ewaso Nyiro.

Be sure to visit our spa, our photography hide and our sundowner deck, and not forgetting our rim-flow swimming pool facing the Koitogo Hills, with Mount Ololokwe to the right. All of these magical touches will make your stay more memorable.

Our team, which consists mainly of indigenous Samburu, is dedicated to offering you the best service and cuisine, using the local produce available to us.

Have you ever slept in the open to see the beautiful Samburu sky? If not, why not try the Star-bed experience?

Soroi Larsens Camp is an eco-friendly camp committed to sustainable tourism and environmental conservation, ensuring the local community benefits from our presence.

We are delighted that you have chosen Soroi Larsens Camp as your destination in the beautiful Samburu National Reserve, and we will endeavour to go above and beyond to ensure your stay is nothing less than exceptional.

Umoja Women's Village

Discover a unique matriarchal village near Archer's Post in Samburu County. Founded in 1990, this community is a sanctuary for women escaping abuse. The village champions women's rights, campaigns against female genital mutilation (FGM) and shelters at-risk girls. Residents support themselves through traditional handcraft production. A 2-hour visit.

Singing Well

Witness the mesmerising Singing Wells ritual, a cornerstone of Samburu culture. Samburu warriors form a living chain, descending into deep wells carved from parched riverbeds. Their harmonious chants echo across the landscape, coordinating their movements as they pass water upwards, bucket by bucket. 14 km, about 30 minutes' drive.

USD 60Per person, min. 2

Samburu Beadwork: Artistry in Threads

The colours of the beads hold specific meanings: red signifies bravery and unity, while blue evokes energy and sustenance. Geometric patterns and sequences mirror rhythm. It is more than art, it is a language of history, affiliation and dreams. In every piece the Samburu people spin their cultural identity.

Little Adventurers

Soroi Kids Club

Welcome to the Soroi Kids Club, where young adventurers embark on thrilling journeys through the wild. At our club, children can unleash their imagination and curiosity in a variety of exciting activities. Activities inside the Soroi Kids Club tent are suitable for all ages.

Samburu’s Creation

A guided "smelly mocktail party". Children go and find lots of interesting smells, make a mocktail, give it a name, then gather to share it and present the name to the other guests.

Samburu Snapshots

It is easy to miss the small things in nature. Our naturalist gives each child a special "camera" to take snapshots of the amazing things around them, then share their discoveries with their friends.

Map Sticks

How do Samburu warriors find their way around the bush without a map and compass? Tie pieces of string and small objects to a long stick as you walk, to remind you of the landmarks on your path, then tell a friend the story of your journey and see if they can follow it.

Bird Spotting

Learn about adaptation: why birds have different beaks (nectar, flesh and seed eaters) and why certain animals, such as the Special 5, can survive only in certain areas.

Bows & Arrows

Young adventurers learn bush survival techniques, going into the bush with the animator to cut and make bows and arrows, then aiming at a target to earn points.

Bush Walks

A nature walk from reception to the water point. Young explorers discover Samburu birds, animal tracks and wilderness signs, learn to make a natural toothbrush and build their animal tracking skills.

Wildlife Reporter

Children act as reporters for Animal Planet and write an interest or travel article on Larsens, its wildlife or the Samburu people.

Cultural Activities

Young boys join the warriors to learn more about the culture and how to make fire using sticks. Those staying more than 3 to 4 days can join the village, learn the roles of men and women, herd the animals and dance with the warriors.

Animal Presentation

Children choose an animal and learn as much as they can about it with the help of the naturalist, then give a talk to teach the other kids.

Wood & Sandstone Carving

Young rangers aged 13 to 17 can join the team in making carvings of animals and people.

Crazy Animals

Draw an animal's head, add another animal's neck, then another's body, legs, tail and patterns. The first letters of the animals give the crazy animal its crazy name.

We always ask that an adult accompany children, and trust parents and guardians to keep a watchful eye on them.

Beyond the Camp

Explore Samburu

Soroi Larsens Camp sits on and along the Ewaso Nyiro River in Samburu National Reserve, with a rim-flow pool facing the Koitogo Hills and Mount Ololokwe to the right. Your guide, the Camp Manager and the team will help you shape each day.

Samburu wildlife, filmed around camp. Tap play.

01

Samburu Special 5

These animals contribute to the diverse and fascinating wildlife of the Samburu National Reserve.

Somali Ostrich

The Somali ostrich (Struthio molybdophanes) is a subspecies of ostrich native to the Horn of Africa, including Samburu National Reserve. Its distinctive blue-grey neck and legs suit the arid environment of the region.

Reticulated Giraffe

The reticulated giraffe (Giraffa camelopardalis reticulata) is known for its striking coat of large, polygonal patches that resemble a net or web. Found in northern Kenya, its pattern helps it blend into the surrounding acacia trees.

Beisa Oryx

The beisa oryx (Oryx beisa) is a large antelope of the arid and semi-arid regions of East Africa, with distinctively long, straight horns that curve slightly backwards. It is well adapted to desert conditions and can survive without water for long periods.

Grevy’s Zebra

Grevy's zebra (Equus grevyi) is the largest and most endangered zebra species, known for narrower stripes than other zebras and a white underbelly. Adapted to arid habitats, it is recognised by its larger ears and more donkey-like appearance.

Gerenuk

The gerenuk (Litocranius walleri) is a unique antelope with a long neck and legs, allowing it to reach high leaves and branches in the acacia trees that other antelopes cannot access. The name means "giraffe-necked" in Somali.

Safety & Surroundings

Please Read

An Unfenced Camp

Soroi Larsens Camp is a unique experience, as it is not fenced. While guards protect the camp 24/7, we recommend vigilance, as wild animals surround you. Your safety is our priority.

01

At night — our askari (watchman) will accompany you around camp.

02

Please do not walk alone at night. Soroi Larsens Camp does not accept liability if you disregard these guidelines. Call Reception on +254 111 936 625 if you need any assistance.

Procedure in Case of Fire

01

Exit — Leave the tent immediately and move to a clear area. Do not stop to pick up personal belongings or attempt to salvage anything.

02

Report — Report any fire to a staff member immediately.

03

Assemble — Proceed to the assembly point at the camp entrance for a head count.

04

Wait — Do not re-enter until management has declared it safe.
